# Federal Reserve Rate Decision: March 2026

## Summary

The Federal Reserve held its benchmark interest rate steady at 4.25%-4.50% at its March 2026 meeting, as widely expected by markets. Chair Jerome Powell emphasized that the committee remains data-dependent and is in no rush to cut rates despite growing political pressure from the White House.

The decision was unanimous among voting members, marking the fourth consecutive meeting without a rate change. The Fed's updated dot plot showed a median expectation of two rate cuts by year-end, down from three projected in December.

## Economic Outlook

The Fed revised its GDP growth forecast down to 1.7% for 2026, from 2.1% previously, citing uncertainty around trade policy and the ongoing conflict in the Middle East. Inflation projections were revised upward, with core PCE expected to remain at 2.8% through the end of the year.

Labor market conditions remain solid but are showing signs of cooling. The unemployment rate ticked up to 4.2% in February, and job openings have declined for three consecutive months. Average hourly earnings growth has moderated to 3.4% year-over-year.

## Market Reaction

Treasury yields fell sharply following the announcement, with the 10-year yield dropping 12 basis points to 4.15%. The S&P 500 rallied 1.2% as investors interpreted the dovish tone as a signal that rate cuts remain on the table for the second half of the year.

The dollar weakened against major currencies, with the DXY index falling below 104 for the first time since January. Gold prices surged above $2,900 per ounce.

## Trade Policy Impact

Powell specifically addressed the impact of tariffs on inflation expectations, noting that recent trade actions have introduced "meaningful uncertainty" into the outlook. The Fed's models suggest that the cumulative tariff increases announced since February could add 0.3-0.5 percentage points to core inflation over the next 12 months.

"We are monitoring the situation closely," Powell said. "Trade policy developments are among the most significant sources of uncertainty we face."

## Housing Market

The housing market continues to struggle under the weight of elevated mortgage rates. The average 30-year fixed rate remains above 6.5%, and existing home sales fell 5.2% in February to their lowest level since 2012.

New housing starts have been more resilient, supported by builder incentives and a chronic shortage of inventory. However, affordability remains the primary constraint, with the median home price-to-income ratio at historic highs in most metropolitan areas.
